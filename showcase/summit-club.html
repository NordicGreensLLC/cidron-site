Day 1: Departure From Content

Expedition log, day one. We set out at dawn from the last known piece of content, a modest header sitting at the foot of the page, and began the long climb toward a destination that, according to all available maps, was intentionally left blank.1 Morale is high. The team has packed light: one headline, a subtitle, and approximately fourteen hundred words of filler, which we are told is the lightest known substance that still counts as luggage.

The terrain ahead is rugged. There are ridges on every horizon, and each ridge, upon reaching it, reveals another ridge, which we are choosing to read as encouragement. Our guide, a very serious paragraph, assures us the summit is within reach. He has also assured us that it is blank. We have decided to trust him on both counts, because he has the compass and we have snacks.

“We do not climb because it is there. We climb because the page was blank and we felt awkward just standing around.”

Day 2: The Long Ascent Into Filler

Day two. Progress is steady, if you define progress as moving sideways while scenery drifts past at several different speeds.2 The nearest mountains slide by quickly, the middle mountains take their time, and the far mountains seem not to be moving at all, which is a lie, because we measured them with a ruler and they are doing about five pixels per second. The team is learning patience. The mule, Kevin, has been told that patience is a virtue. Kevin has not been listening.3

At midday we reached a plateau and stopped for lunch, which was a long paragraph about lunch. We ate it quietly, looking out over a landscape of absolutely no information. Somebody suggested putting a chart here to break up the monotony. Somebody else pointed out that the chart would also be blank, which brought the group to a respectful silence that lasted until the cursor changed shape, which it does every ten seconds, and which startled the entire expedition.

Day 3: Equipment Inventory

On the third day we took stock of our supplies, as every disciplined expedition does when morale needs a distraction. Our inventory is as follows: one rope, forty feet, used for nothing. Two ice axes, symbolic. A tent, in which nobody slept, because the tent is also blank. Seven flashlights, none needed, because this page is in dark mode and has been the entire time. One thermos of hot cocoa, which we looked for and could not find, and which we are now treating as a missing person.4

We also carry a compass that points reliably toward the footnotes, a first-aid kit containing a single sticker, and a pocket-sized copy of the previous page, which we are reading for tips on how to say nothing at greater length. So far it has taught us that adverbs are free and that sentences, like trails, are best when they take far too long to arrive. We have taken this advice to heart. Hence this paragraph.

“Pack light, write long.” — The Expedition Handbook, page 0

Day 4: The Summit (Also Blank)

Day four. We have reached the summit. The view is, to put it mildly, a lot of nothing. In every direction there are more ridges, a pleasant sunset, and a sky that appears to have been painted by someone who ran out of ideas and decided to commit to purple. We planted a flag. The flag is blank.5 We took a team photo. The photo is blank. We shook hands, and the handshake was blank, in that nobody could work out how long it was supposed to last.

Standing at the top, we asked ourselves the difficult questions. Why did we climb this? What were we hoping to find? Is there a gift shop? And the mountain, in the way that only mountains can, said nothing at all, which in this case is the correct answer, because this page is intentionally left blank, and the mountain, as it turns out, was also in on it.

“The summit is just a very tall place to be disappointed.” — Kevin the mule

Day 5: Descent and Reflection

The descent was uneventful, in the sense that nothing happened, which is the theme of the expedition and, indeed, the page. On the way down we reflected on what we had learned. We learned that mountains are very tall. We learned that filler travels well. We learned that a determined writer can get four paragraphs out of a single hike and a cocoa that never existed. The team is exhausted, but in good spirits, and the mule is fine.6

If you have followed us all the way from base camp, we salute you. You have scrolled a very long way to read about a place that does not exist, in the company of strangers who are also not real. As a reward, we would like to show you the part of this page that is not a joke: what these effects actually are, and how you can bring the same sense of motion and polish to your own website. What you’re actually looking at

This page is a live demo of visual enhancements: animated touches that Cidron adds to a small business website so it looks polished and memorable.

Two moving backgrounds

The background slides sideways in an endless loop and swaps to a second one about every 26 seconds.

  • Mountain Ranges: four ridgelines, pine trees and mist sliding past at different speeds against a sunset.
  • Sliding Tile Rows: rows of colored triangles gliding sideways like conveyor belts, with a wave of light running through them.

A cursor effect that changes every 10 seconds

On a computer it follows your mouse. On a phone, touch the screen and drag. When nobody is touching anything it drifts on its own so you can watch it. The name of the current effect is shown at the bottom of the screen.

  • Ember Trail: glowing embers rise from your cursor.
  • Targeting Lens: a precision ring and crosshair that tightens as you move.
  • Chevron Trail: an arrow that points where you are heading, leaving a trail of arrowheads.
  • Paint Splats: splashes of paint land wherever you go.
  • Falling Leaves: autumn leaves tumble down from your cursor.

Everything is built from scratch in your brand’s colors, runs smoothly on phones, and calms itself down automatically for visitors who prefer less motion.
